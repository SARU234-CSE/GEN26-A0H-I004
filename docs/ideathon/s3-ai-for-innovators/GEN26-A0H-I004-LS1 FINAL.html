Abstract

This chapter introduces an AI-managed edge-fog-cloud continuum to privacy-friendly healthcare intelligence, which combines federated learning with care environments powered by the metaverse. The framework allows training the model in a decentralized way within diverse healthcare networks and still preserve the data locality and regulatory control. Data processing on an edge device helps to achieve real-time results, a fog node helps to organize the aggregation and optimization of resources, and cloud layers help to optimize global models and analytics. Secure aggregation, differential privacy, and trusted execution environments are privacy-enhancing methods that provide strong data protection. The case-based analysis has shown an increase in predictive accuracy, patient engagement and efficiency in collaboration. The results also indicate that federated intelligence and immersive healthcare environments can be used to create scalable, reliable, and patient-centered virtual care environments.

Introduction

AI orchestration in healthcare is described as an intelligent synchronization of data computations, model training, and decision-making processes among distributed levels of computation, in order to provide efficient, scalable and privacy conscious intelligence. The edge-fog-cloud continuum in this context can be seen as a unified architecture paradigm with data being generated and processed on the edge (e.g. in IoMT devices and local hospital system), aggregated and orchestrated at the fog layer (e.g. in institutional servers), and optimization in the cloud infrastructures at the global scale. This type of continuum guarantees resource-efficiency, low-latency responsiveness and integrated heterogeneous healthcare settings. In this context, federated learning plays the role of the central orchestration machine because it allows decentralizing model training and sharing intelligence in a synchronized fashion without exposing sensitive patient information (Bajwa et al., 2021). It is dynamic in terms of controlling learning workflows throughout the continuum, enabling collaborative intelligence, locality of data, and compliance of regulation, thus forming a sound base of scalable and reliable AI-driven healthcare ecosystems.

Healthcare Data Fragmentation Challenges

The problem of fragmentation of healthcare data continues to be a major obstacle toward successful digital transformation. Patient data is spread across hospitals, laboratories, wearable devices, imaging systems and national registries, usually incompatible with each other and with varying policies applied to them. This division restricts the analysis of longitudinal patients, limits the population-level intelligence, and decreases the trustworthiness of AI-based clinical knowledge. Moreover, privacy laws, complications in management of consent and institutional risk avoidance often act as hindrances to cross- institutional data sharing (Hiran et al., 2024b; Lehne et al., 2019; Ramasamy & Doshi, 2022). With the rise in the amount, speed, and diversity of multimodal data in healthcare, these issues are exacerbated by the increased adoption of information intensive technologies in healthcare. New health applications (such as real-time monitoring and predictive analytics) require constant access to various datasets and very high confidentiality. The lack of privacy-aware and interoperable data infrastructures leads to redundancy of efforts, biased models and underutilized clinical knowledge (Rieke et al., 2020). Fragmentation can only be addressed by ensuring technical integration, but it is also necessary to have governance systems to balance innovation and accountability, trust, and compliance across distributed healthcare ecosystems.

AI-Driven Virtual and Metaverse Healthcare

AI-based virtual care is expanding as healthcare systems seek scalable, personalized, and continuous care solutions. With the implementation of metaverse technologies, there will be immersive experiences where patients, clinicians and intelligent agents can interact in continuing virtual worlds. The supported apps are virtual hospitals, digital twins of facilities and patients, immersive teleconsultations, and remote clinical training. Under such circumstances, the AI models can provide real-time analytics, predictive diagnostics and adaptive treatment courses. However, metaverse healthcare is a more immersive environment, and it adds new challenges to the topic of identity management, data sovereignty, and algorithmic accountability. Behavioral cues, sensors, and avatars with streams of constant data make the interactions in the virtual world more sensitive and reveal personal health information. Thus, metaverse healthcare that is powered by AI has a chance of success with the assistance of the architectures that will ensure the safety of the data processing, its clarity, and user trustworthiness (Hiran et al., 2024a; Ravikumar, Aarthi, Sapayev, et al., 2025). Solutions to privacy that protect the immersive virtual care promise should be implemented to make sure that the ethical, legal, and social concerns do not affect the promise of immersive virtual care.
